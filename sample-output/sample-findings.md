# Findings for the sample shipment

Schema check: passes entry.xsd

## Fix before filing

- **Importer tax ID is needed.** No shipping document carries the importer of record's EIN or CBP-assigned number, and NetCHB needs it to find the importer. _Next: Enter the importer's tax ID (for example 12-3456789)._
- **Bond information is needed.** Bond type and surety code are not on any shipping document. Most consumption entries need a continuous or single-transaction bond. _Next: Enter the bond type (08 continuous, 09 single transaction) and surety code._
- **5 tariff codes need the full 10-digit HTSUS number.** The documents only give 6-digit HS codes (6109.10, 6110.20, 6203.42, 6206.40, 6106.10). US entries need the 10-digit HTSUS code, which depends on details like gender, fibre content and knit versus woven. The entry currently carries the 6-digit codes. _Next: Have the broker confirm the 10-digit HTSUS code for each line, then enter it in the form._
- **Manufacturer ID (MID) is needed for each producer.** The MID is a CBP code built from the factory's name and address. It is not printed on any document. Factories seen: SAIGON PHOENIX GARMENT JSC; SP Garments Dhaka Ltd.. The sample garments come from a different factory in a different country, so they need their own MID. _Next: Enter the MID for each line in the form._

## Review

- **Container number was changed by hand on the bill of lading.** The printed number OPLU3041728 has an invalid check digit (it should end in 2). The handwritten correction reads OPLU3041722, which has a valid check digit (the handwriting is hard to read). The entry uses OPLU3041722. _Next: Confirm the final container number with the carrier or forwarder before filing._
- **Entry type 01 (consumption, free and dutiable) was assumed.** The documents do not say how the goods are being entered. Type 01 is the usual choice for commercial imports but it is an assumption. _Next: Confirm the entry type._
- **NORTHBOUND OUTFITTERS, INC. was assumed to be the importer of record.** The documents name the buyer and notify party but never say who is the importer of record. _Next: Confirm the importer of record._
- **Goods are delivered to a different company than the buyer.** The bill of lading consignee and the invoice ship-to are CASCADE FULFILLMENT PARTNERS LLC, delivering "for account of" NORTHBOUND OUTFITTERS, INC.. The ultimate consignee was set to NORTHBOUND OUTFITTERS, INC., and CASCADE FULFILLMENT PARTNERS LLC is treated as the warehouse. _Next: Confirm which company is the ultimate consignee._
- **An invoice line amount looks mistyped.** NB-T101 shows 6,480.00 but 2400 x 2.85 = 6,840.00. The printed lines add up to 22,875.00, but the invoice total of 23,235.00 only works with the recalculated amount, so the entry uses 6,840.00. _Next: Ask the seller to confirm the corrected line amount._
- **Goods come from more than one country.** NB-P118S is BD (invoice line note), while the rest of the shipment is VN. Shipment-wide statements say otherwise (invoice declaration: VIETNAM; bill of lading marks: made in VIETNAM). The entry uses line-level origin, because origin drives duty rates. _Next: Confirm the origin of each style with the seller. The invoice declaration and bill of lading marks should be corrected if they are wrong._
- **Possible assist: 4,180.00 of buyer-supplied material.** Main fabric, 100% polyester crepe de chine, 1,450 m, supplied free of charge by buyer (buyer P.O. NB-26-0918F) was supplied free by the buyer and is not in the invoice price. US customs rules generally treat materials the buyer supplies to the producer as an assist that can be added to the dutiable value. It was not added: W-B220 is entered at 1,260.00; adding it would make 5,440.00. _Next: Decide with the broker whether to add the assist, then use the toggle in the form._
- **Fibre content differs for NB-H205.** The invoice says 60% Cotton 40% Polyester brushed fleece (60/40); the packing list says "LADIES PULLOVER HOODIE TC 65/35 FLEECE 280GSM" (65/35). "TC" normally means polyester-cotton, usually with polyester first. Fibre content can change the tariff code, for example cotton versus man-made fibre. _Next: Ask the factory for the actual fabric composition before choosing the HTSUS code._
- **Quantities differ between invoice and packing list.** NB-H205: invoice 1200 pcs, packing list 1176 pcs (-24, about 177.60 at the invoice price). The overall piece totals look equal (5100), but only because the missing pieces offset the free samples. The totals agree even though the styles do not. The entry uses the invoice quantity and price. _Next: Confirm what was actually shipped. If fewer pieces shipped, the entered value changes too._
- **Gross weight differs between bill of lading and packing list.** Bill of lading: 1930 kg. Packing list: 1888 kg (42 kg more on the bill of lading). Cartons are not palletised, so pallets do not explain it. The entry header uses the bill of lading weight, as that is what the carrier manifested. _Next: Ask the forwarder whether the bill of lading includes packaging or a different weighing._

## Good to know

- **Bill numbers were split from their carrier codes.** Printed master bill OPLUSGN260917735 became carrier code OPLU + bill SGN260917735; printed house bill BMLVHCM26090418 became BMLV + HCM26090418. This follows the usual convention that the first letters are the carrier's SCAC. _Next: Check against the AMS/ACE manifest record if the bill numbers are rejected._
- **Customs broker is not named yet.** The notify party on the bill of lading lists the customs broker as "TBA". _Next: Make sure the filing broker is confirmed and that the carrier/forwarder has their details._
- **Processing port was set to the entry port.** The processing port is the broker's own filing port and is not on any shipping document. _Next: Change it in the form if your office files elsewhere._
- **Entry date was set to the estimated arrival date.** The invoice gives an estimated arrival of 2026-10-14. The real entry date depends on when the broker files. _Next: Set the entry date when you file._
- **Invoice number written as KBASNB26-0912.** NetCHB only accepts letters, digits and hyphens (up to 17 characters), so KBAS/NB/26-0912 was shortened to KBASNB26-0912. _Next: Keep the original invoice number in your own records._
- **Goods are sold CIF, so freight and insurance are kept out of entered value.** Entered values use the goods-only total (23,235.00). Freight and insurance of 4,035.00 are reported as header charges, as an assumption about how NetCHB uses that field. _Next: Check the header charges field against your office practice._
- **Free samples are included.** NB-P118S (24 pcs) are free of charge. They were entered at the stated customs value ($24.00) and kept out of the invoice total, as the invoice says. _Next: Confirm how your office treats free samples._
- **Buyer and seller relationship is not stated.** The entry asks whether buyer and seller are related. The documents do not say, so the field was left out. _Next: Confirm whether the parties are related._
- **The seller is not the manufacturer.** KOWLOON BAY APPAREL SOURCING LIMITED (Hong Kong sourcing office) invoices goods made by SAIGON PHOENIX GARMENT JSC and SP Garments Dhaka Ltd.. The MID and country of origin describe the maker, not the seller.
- **Check that the importer security filing (ISF) is on file.** The bill of lading says the ISF is filed by "IMPORTER/AGENT". US ocean imports need an ISF before loading (this cargo was loaded on 2026-09-24), and NetCHB warns before transmitting if none is found. _Next: Confirm the ISF with the importer or their agent._
- **Bill of lading is telex released.** Original bills were surrendered at origin, so the cargo can be released without paper originals.
- **Duties and fees are not calculated here.** NetCHB calculates duties and fees from the tariff codes unless the entry is flagged as precalculated. This tool leaves that to NetCHB.
- **The entry is not set to transmit to customs.** The XML has no transmit tag, so nothing is sent to CBP when it is uploaded.
